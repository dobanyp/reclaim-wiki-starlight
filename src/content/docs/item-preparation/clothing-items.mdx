Alongside store organization, one of the main tasks we do on a daily basis is the item preparation. When we are working with clothing items ordered by one of our big suppliers, all the garments should go through a 5 step preparation pipeline. The main steps are:

1. Hanging
2. Ironing & Quality checking
3. Laundering
4. Fixing
5. Security tagging
6. Pricing & Price tagging
7. Putting away

We keep track of the progress of certain categories by the Progress Tracker Sheet, which needs to be filled out every time by the staff member who starts or finishes any of the steps above. **Please don't forget to log your progress in the [Progress Tracker Sheet](#) at all times!**

## Hanging

<Steps>

1. Bring a rack - preferably one in an acceptable condition - to the laundry room with the appropriate type of hangers for the category.
2. Open the bags appointed by the store manager, and cross out the name of the category from the inventory list.
   <Aside>
     If you find multiple bags with the same SKU, open all the bags unless the
     store manager or Loi / Dorka doesn't tell otherwise. If you're in doubt,
     always ask a store manager before proceeding.
   </Aside>
3. Cut out or peel off the bag sticker with the category name and SKU number, stick it on an empty hanger, and place the hanger at the beginning of the rack.
4. Hang the items.
5. Bring the full rack back to the ironing room.

</Steps>

## Ironing & Quality Checking

<Steps>
1. Check if the ironing machine is filled with enough distilled water. If not, fill up it's water tank.
2. Iron or steam the garments according to their fabric type and care requirements. 
3. During ironing / steaming, inspect every item for damages and stains. Observe the seams, edges, buttons and zippers especially thoroughly.
4. If you find a stain on an item, try to remove it with a baby wipe first. If the stain seems tougher, put a paper note on the hanger indicating that the item should go to the laundry.
5. If an item smells bad, try to fix it with the textile spray. If it doesn't help much, or the garment smells exceptionally nasty, mark it as "laundry" as well.
6. In case you notice a tear or a damage, mark it with a colored round sticker, and put a paper note on the hanger detailing the issue (damage type, location, etc). 
7. Zip and button up all garments after ironing. If you notice a damaged or missing button, zipper, or zipper pull, write a paper note and slip it over the neck of the hanger.
8. If the item has pockets, don't forget to check them as well. Use a latex glove to avoid unpleasant surprises.
9. When you are done with the inspection of the items, bring the laundry to the laundry room.
10. Log your progress in the Progress Tracker Sheet.

</Steps>

<Aside title="Thoroughness over speed">
  A general rule of thumb for ironing & QC is **thoroughness over speed.** Don't
  rush the process, and take all the time you need to spot every damage and
  stain.
</Aside>

### Ironing: Tips, tricks and special cases

- For leather and suede jackets, steam the lining only. Keep it light, and
  avoid steaming the outer material directly.
- Steam the nylon raincoats
  especially carefully, to avoid accidental melting.
- Viscose shirts smooth out
  beatifully with light steaming alone. You don't have to necessarily iron them
  on the ironing board.
- The hardest and most unforgiving categories are the
  shirts, the trenches, and the linen blouses / pants. Don't rush them, take
  your time with the ironing. It is normal that they take a really long time.
- If you are in doubt, don't hesitate to take a note, either if you spot some
  irregularity on the item, you suspect that something is missing from it, or
  even if you couldn't iron it properly because of the material (it happens). It
  is always better to document more than to miss a problem. - You don't have to
  iron the items which need to be washed!

## Laundering

<Steps>
1. Remove every colored sticker, price tag and security tag from the items, and also check the pockets before putting them in the washing machine.
2. Put the clothes in the washing machine in small batches, sorted by color. 
3. Put a color catching sheet in the machine alongside the colored clothes.
4. Select the appropriate wash cycle settings according to the material of the item. (The more delicate materials should be washed at lower temperatures, etc. **NEVER put wool items in the washing machine!**)
5. Set a reminder for yourself to hang the laundry when the washing cycle ends.
6. Hang the laundry on the rack dedicated for the drying laundry.
7. Log your progress in the Progress Tracker Sheet.

</Steps>

## Fixing

Based on your notes, the store manager will make a decision whether an item is fixable or not. If the item is considered fixable, the store manager will appoint the fixing task to one of the staff members who are skilled at sewing. For example, missing buttons are always fixable damages, while items that are torn anywhere but the seam are usually non-repairables.

## Re-ironing

When the laundry dried properly, we need to iron them again. You can follow the same steps during reironing as for the initial ironing, except if the item is still stained, mark the stain with a colored sticker, and slip a paper note on the neck of the hanger with a short description of the stain (type, location).

## Security Tagging

You can start the security tagging after all the items are collected together from the laundering and the fixing.

<Steps>
1. Put the pin in the seam of the item, and push the security tag firmly on the pin. Avoid sticking the pin through the fabric, always try to minimize the damage by placing the needle through the seam as precisely as possible.

2. Check if the security tag is staying on the item and not falling off with a slight pull.

</Steps>

### Sec-tagging: Things to pay attention to

- Try to place the tag on an easily reachable place to make the de-tagging easier for the cashier.

- If possible, place the pin between the lining and the outer material, and avoid pinning through the lining.

- Avoid double tagging, so make sure that the item has not been already sec-tagged.

- Make sure that the placement of the sec tag does not affect the customer when they try the item on.

- If you mess up to tagging, you can always remove them with the de-tagging magnet in the ironing room.

## Price Tagging

Loi or Dorka slides the price tags on the neck of the hangers. After pricing, we have to put the price tags on the items itself.

Generally, you should attach the price tags to the items using the **white or black price tag threads**, threading them through the hanger loops, non-functional buttonholes, belt loops, or any other kind of hole or loop that serves a decorative purpose or is not likely to be used when the customer tries on the item. Preferably, we should avoid placing the price tag in the main buttonholes and zippers on pants and shirts, because they tend to fall off in the fitting room.

For items without any loops or holes, you should attach the price tags with the **pricing gun**. Try to stick the needle/applier of the gun through the seam of the item, similarly to how you would with security tagging.

The items you typically use the pricing gun on:

- Shirts & T-shirts
- Blouses & tops
- Bathing suits & swimming shorts
- Hoodies, cardigans & sweaters

## Putting the stock away

When the preparation is finished, the store manager will decide whether to fill the store up with the prepared garments, or put them away in the back stock.

When you place any items in the stock, make sure that you put the items under the proper category. Check for any labels matching the category you intend to place in the stock. If you can't find any, write a new one using the white plastic cards and the colored IKEA markers placed on the big white table in the ironing room.

<Aside type="caution">
  **NEVER** use permanent markers on the white plastic cards.
</Aside>
